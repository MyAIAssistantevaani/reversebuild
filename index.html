THE REVERSEBUILD METHOD

Don't just ask whether an idea is good. Understand how it could work.

ReverseBuild is designed around a simple principle: study businesses solving a similar problem, understand the mechanics behind their model, and then work backward from your own target.

01

Find comparable businesses

Identify businesses with meaningful similarities in customer, problem, product, market, or business model.

02

Study their strategy

Examine customer positioning, product structure, pricing, acquisition channels, traffic sources, conversion paths, and growth mechanisms.

03

Work backward from your goal

Turn a revenue target into customer, conversion, lead, traffic, and acquisition requirements using explicit assumptions and calculations.

04

Decide what to change

Separate strategies worth keeping from areas that deserve improvement or alternative experiments.

THE DECISION LAYER

Not everything needs to be copied.

A successful business can provide useful evidence without providing a perfect blueprint. ReverseBuild separates observation from recommendation.

Each strategic component can be classified as KEEP, IMPROVE, or CHANGE, with the reasoning and assumptions made visible.

KEEP

The comparable strategy appears relevant to the user's circumstances and is worth considering without major changes.

IMPROVE

The underlying mechanism may be useful, but there is an opportunity to test a stronger execution.

CHANGE

The comparable approach may not fit the user's constraints, creating a reason to test another route.

BUSINESS PLANNING

What is revenue backcasting?

Revenue backcasting is a business planning method that starts with a specific financial target and works backward to identify the customers, conversions, traffic, and activities that could be required to pursue it.

Start with the outcome

Instead of beginning with a list of marketing activities, define the result you want to achieve. For example, you might set a target of $10,000 in monthly revenue.

Work backward through the funnel

Once the target is defined, estimate the number of customers required based on your pricing. Then use an assumed conversion rate to estimate the amount of website traffic or qualified demand that may be needed.

Turn assumptions into experiments

These calculations are not predictions. They are planning assumptions that can be tested against real customer behavior, conversion data, acquisition costs, retention, and sales performance.

Why revenue backcasting matters for a new business

A business idea can sound attractive without having a clear path to customers and revenue. Backcasting creates a measurable connection between the desired outcome and the operating assumptions behind it.

For example, a $10,000 monthly revenue goal means very different things for a $10 product, a $100 subscription, and a $1,000 service. Pricing changes the number of customers required, while conversion assumptions influence the amount of demand or traffic that may need to enter the funnel.

ReverseBuild combines this type of quantitative planning with business model analysis, comparable-business research, customer acquisition analysis, pricing strategy, conversion funnel analysis, and growth planning.

The goal is not to tell you that a business idea will succeed. The goal is to make the assumptions behind the business easier to see, test, and improve.
